## NASA solar-system hero imagery

The homepage uses locally compressed WebP derivatives of NASA imagery. Credit
remains with the agencies and mission teams listed below. The `*-cutout.webp`
files are transparent-background presentation derivatives of the corresponding
sources. Earth, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn cutouts were isolated or
completed with an OpenAI image-editing pass followed by local chroma-key
removal; the other four cutouts use local background extraction. These
derivatives are decorative and must not be treated as new scientific source
imagery.

- `planets/mercury.webp`: MESSENGER globe, NASA/Johns Hopkins University APL/
  Carnegie Institution of Washington. Source:
  https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/
- `planets/venus.webp`: Magellan radar-color globe, NASA/JPL-Caltech. Source:
  https://science.nasa.gov/venus/
- `planets/earth.webp`: Blue Marble imagery, NASA Goddard Space Flight Center.
  Source: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/
- `planets/mars.webp`: Viking global view, NASA/JPL/USGS. Source:
  https://science.nasa.gov/mars/
- `planets/jupiter.webp`: Juno enhanced-color view, NASA/JPL-Caltech/SwRI/MSSS.
  Source: https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/
- `planets/saturn.webp`: Cassini approach view, NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute.
  Source: https://science.nasa.gov/image-detail/pia05380-saturn-with-rings-16x9/
- `planets/uranus.webp`: Voyager 2 view, NASA/JPL-Caltech. Source:
  https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/
- `planets/neptune.webp`: Voyager 2 view, NASA/JPL. Source:
  https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/
- `planets/pluto.webp`: New Horizons natural-color view, NASA/Johns Hopkins
  University APL/Southwest Research Institute. Source:
  https://science.nasa.gov/resource/pluto-in-high-resolution/
- `asteroids/bennu.webp` and derived transparent `asteroids/bennu-cutout.webp`:
  OSIRIS-REx view of Bennu, NASA/Goddard/University of
  Arizona. Source: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/101955-bennu/
- `asteroids/itokawa.webp` and derived transparent `asteroids/itokawa-cutout.webp`:
  Hayabusa view of Itokawa, JAXA. Source:
  https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/25143-itokawa/

NASA media usage guidelines:
https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/
